# Comparable games and adjacent markets

[Analysis index](README.md) · [Audiences](audiences.md) · [Jobs to be done](jobs-to-be-done.md) · [Channels and business models](channels-and-business-models.md) · [Product facts](../facts/product.md)

Research checked **2026-10-26**. This is a possibility map, not a ranked competitor list, market-size estimate or positioning recommendation. External product descriptions below come from developer/publisher pages and their storefront listings. The **comparison and motivation columns are analyst interpretations**. Comparable mechanics do not establish shared buyers, substitutability, willingness to pay or likely acquisition costs. No comparative playtest was performed.

## Glob2 baseline and axes

The [product ledger](../facts/product.md) documents indirect building demand and flags, autonomous globs with food/health/training needs, colony economy, rival matches, AI, campaigns, replays, editor and source-level creator systems. It also separates optional Studio/payment infrastructure from verified production services. [Distribution](../facts/distribution.md) separates browser early access, experimental native builds and older packages. This baseline is conditional on version, ruleset and deployed feature configuration.

The useful axes are **who acts** (individual orders, building demand, incentives or code), **what flows** (workers, food, materials, production), **what threatens progress** (opponents, survival or efficiency), **how play ends** (match, scenario, ongoing colony or persistent world), **what players create**, and **what the payment buys**. None is a universal measure of quality.

## Code and creator adjacency

**Screeps: World** is an additional comparison for the source-level Glob2 JavaScript AI systems, not a substitute for the core game. Its [official store](https://store.screeps.com/world) describes persistent, 24/7 JavaScript-controlled colonies, logistics and territorial conflict, with a CPU-limited starter allowance and paid CPU unlock mechanisms. It also allows self-hosted servers. Glob2’s AI scripting and validation could plausibly interest some code-oriented experimenters, but authored bots in discrete matches are different from maintaining a persistent MMO colony. Programming is the central Screeps interaction; Glob2 does not require users to program to play. No shared audience or paid-compute demand has been measured.

Its [creator program](https://store.screeps.com/creators) also documents affiliate links, replays and casting opportunities. That is evidence of one publisher’s channel design, not a recommendation to adopt it or evidence that it works economically for Glob2. Likewise, generative assistance, authored AI opponents, conventional computer opponents and content creation are distinct propositions; the word “AI” does not make them one market.

## Possibilities and tradeoffs exposed by the comparisons

| Possible comparison frame | What it might help communicate — hypothesis | Expectation or disadvantage it might introduce | Evidence needed to distinguish alternatives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Indirect RTS / autonomous actors | Where decisions occur when every unit is not directly commanded. | May imply weak agency, arbitrary AI or Majesty-style rewards. | Observe first-time players predicting and diagnosing task assignment. |
| Colony economy / living system | Food, labor and expansion interact and create visible consequences. | May imply persistent characters, relationships, survival narrative or a peaceful sandbox. | Ask players what kind of colony continuity and threat they expect from actual footage. |
| Competitive RTS | Opponent pressure, map control, economic tradeoffs and match mastery. | May imply conventional unit control, established ladder population or esports support. | Test control comprehension and match experience; measure online availability separately. |
| Logistics / automation | Bottlenecks and improving a working system. | May imply belts, blueprints or a much broader production-chain game. | Compare responses to actual Glob2 decisions with automation-only expectations. |
| Open-source / long-lived revival | Free access, continuity and contribution possibilities. | May trigger expectations about volunteer support, unfinished software or specific historic builds. | Separate remembered versions from new-player observations and current build reliability. |
| Creator / programmable strategy | Map design, authored opponents and repeatable experiments. | Could overstate live Studio availability or narrow appeal to programmers. | Establish which creator functions are deployed and whether users want creation, coding or assisted generation. |

No frame, peer, channel or monetization pattern is selected here. Missing work includes comparative hands-on sessions, current creator workflow checks, cross-platform performance/accessibility comparisons, attributed player interviews and measured audience overlap. These gaps do not prevent recording the alternatives, but they limit claims about which alternative would work.
