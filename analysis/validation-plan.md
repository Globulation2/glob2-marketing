## Questions and boundaries

The first round asks three separate questions: **What does this make someone expect? Does that experience sound useful or enjoyable to them? Does the actual named build deliver the critical part of that expectation?** A positive answer to one cannot substitute for another. It is not a market-size, retention, pricing or business-model validation study.

Test A (indirect-control strategy), B (colony systems), C (free-software/local-access) and D (authored JavaScript opponents) as separate lead promises. A/B can be compared among players with overlapping motivations. C may be a supporting reason instead of a distinct acquisition message. D needs creator participants and a workflow gate; do not declare it the weakest general-player pitch from responses of non-coders.

## Session procedure and neutral prompts

Proposed duration is 35–45 minutes, with a longer optional creator task if agreed. Offer an unrecorded session or written notes rather than assuming permission to record. Participants can skip a question or stop. Store only de-identified evidence in this public repository; raw contact details, payment details and recordings do not belong here.

1. **Context before exposure (5 minutes).** “Tell me about the last time you chose a strategy game or coding project. What were you trying to do? What else did you consider? What made you stop or return?” Ask for an actual recent example before hypothetical preferences. Do not mention Glob2's proposed audience labels.
2. **Unaided first card (3 minutes).** Show one assigned candidate without explanation. Ask: “What do you think you would actually do? What would the game decide for you? What might a session look like? What would you expect to be free or paid?” Record answers verbatim where useful, with consent. Ask “What in the card suggested that?” rather than correcting immediately.
3. **Expectation probe (3 minutes).** Ask what persists between sessions, what creates pressure or ends play, and what they would need to start. For D ask who writes the code, where it runs and whether programming is necessary for ordinary play. Do not supply answer choices before the open response.
4. **Controlled comparison (5 minutes).** Show a second relevant card, counterbalanced across participants. Ask what changed in their understanding, which circumstance each would fit and what would make them dismiss either. Preserve first-exposure responses; do not count later corrected understanding as unaided comprehension. Not every participant needs all four cards.
5. **Constant proof and observed task (10–15 minutes).** Reveal the same neutral baseline and common gameplay proof. Ask for a prediction before a work-priority change, then an explanation after. In a verified build, invite the participant to achieve the bounded task, think aloud if comfortable, and explain a shortage. Record help separately. For creators, use the qualified edit/test task instead of assuming generic first-play success measures authoring value.
6. **Access and scope (3–5 minutes).** Show the accurate route, account/save limitations and optional-service explanation. Ask what they would do next and why; note whether their interest changes. Do not run a purchase or infer willingness to pay from “sounds fair.”
7. **Debrief (5 minutes).** “What matched or surprised you? What would you use instead? Which part would bring you back, if any? What would prevent another session?” Ask whether their own initial interpretation changed. Finish by correcting material factual misunderstandings; keep these corrections out of initial-comprehension scores.

Counterbalance A/B first exposure and use a preassigned order within each track. Log existing familiarity, message revision, footage/task build and device. Keep comparable production quality and the same amount of explanation. Avoid “Which is best?”, persuading someone through repeated explanations or showing only a candidate's ideal successful state. If product failures prevent the task, label the session affected and investigate; do not silently drop it as an inconvenient response.

## Evidence and coding rules

| Measure | How to record it | Interpretation limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Control comprehension | Before explanation: correctly identifies player demand/flags and autonomous execution, partly correct, or incorrect; retain the actual answer | A declared rating without the answer is not auditable; understanding does not imply enjoyment |
| Expectation mismatch | Record specific expected feature and source cue: direct commands, permanent colony, no combat, short matches, cross-device saves, free optional services, active ladder, required programming, etc. | Do not collapse all mismatches into one appeal score; some need copy revision, others indicate a different desired product |
| Task behavior | Attempt/result, elapsed observation time, unsolicited help, prompted help, prediction and causal explanation | Time is descriptive for this task/build; it is not a claim about total onboarding, accessibility or match length |
| Appeal with reason | Spontaneous desired next action, reason, hesitation, alternative and change after accurate proof | Praise, a 1–5 score or interview politeness is not acquisition or retention |
| Access/offer comprehension | Can explain chosen route, account/save constraints, free core versus optional scoped paid machinery | Understanding does not establish willingness to pay or live service readiness |
| Creator value | Intentional authored change, understood test result, friction, alternative tool and desired next project | Passing validation is not enjoyable play, publication success, creator reach or demand for generative credits |
| Follow-through, if separately agreed | Whether someone voluntarily uses a provided route again after the session, with a stated observation window and consent | No tracking is installed/planned by default. Account-day counts do not identify motivation; lack of observed return can mean incomplete observation |

Use denominators for the relevant track and exposed version, e.g. “3 of 4 first-exposure A sessions,” not “75% of gamers.” Report positive, negative and disconfirming cases together. An affected technical session remains in the log with its limitation; its comprehension evidence may be usable while its task outcome is not. Do not produce a pooled numerical winner across non-equivalent gamer and creator tracks.

### Proposed review criteria

- **Claim gate:** Any central promise unsupported by the named public build/path blocks publication of that wording until corrected or verified. This is a truthfulness gate, not a participant vote.
- **Copy-revision trigger:** The same material false expectation appearing independently in two first-exposure sessions triggers a wording/proof revision and another small check. A single serious mismatch is still recorded and assessed; two is a practical recurrence signal, not a statistical boundary.
- **Comprehension check for a next round:** As a proposed small-round rule, at least three of four relevant first-exposure sessions should explain the central interaction without moderator correction, with no recurring unresolved material mismatch. This only permits carrying a draft forward for further testing; it does not validate the market or approve launch copy.
- **Experience-match check:** Do not advance a promise solely on card appeal when the corresponding qualified gameplay/task consistently changes interest for reasons central to that promise. Show the actual examples and task limitations rather than inventing a universal pass percentage.
- **Audience-fit assessment:** A useful candidate needs specific circumstances, understood value and acknowledged tradeoffs in participant evidence. If explanations differ materially, split or rewrite the hypothesis rather than averaging them into broad enthusiasm.
- **Selection remains separate:** The study report can identify supported/contradicted/uncertain hypotheses and candidate revisions. Choosing the lead audience and launch message requires a review of that evidence and operational capacity; this draft selects neither.

Do not move a criterion after seeing results without documenting why. The small purposive sample cannot estimate market size, revenue, statistical preference, causal conversion lifts or comparative popularity. A later acquisition experiment would need a separate consent/measurement/traffic design and exposure denominators; none is deployed here.
