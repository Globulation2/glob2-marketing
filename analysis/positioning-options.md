Drafted **2026-10-26** following authorization to develop concrete audience/message combinations. These are four **untested candidates**, not a selected positioning, claims approval, or evidence of audience demand. No new player research or current-build playtest was performed. The rows have no preference order. Options A and B describe different motivations for the same core gameplay; C changes the trust/access emphasis; D addresses a separate creator circumstance. A person could respond to several. Each test must distinguish understanding of the game from attraction to a slogan.

## A — Direct the colony

**Audience and occasion.** A strategy player sees autonomous colony footage and wants an unfamiliar way to exercise control. The job is to make consequential economic and military choices without issuing every worker's movement. This is not a claim about low skill, accessibility or reduced total effort.

**Draft headline:** “Set the priorities. The colony gets to work.”

**Draft short pitch:** “Globulation 2 is a free, open-source real-time strategy game where you set worker demand at buildings and place flags. Your globs find their own routes and meet their needs while you balance the colony's work, expansion and rivals.”

**Evidence and proposed proof.** [P01–P03](../facts/product.md#gameplay-and-player-surfaces) document demand/flags, autonomous routing/needs and rival objectives. Existing [A10 tutorial/HUD media](../facts/assets.md#screenshots-recordings-and-educational-media) can ground a test in an actual control example. A proof sequence could show a demand change, the resulting worker response and the competing task left short of labor. That sequence still needs a verified capture; its proposed existence is not a completed asset.

**Value hypothesis.** The meaningful distinction is *where the player intervenes*: setting demand and priorities. [Majesty and conventional RTS comparisons](comparables.md) help explain that distinction, but there is no claim that Glob2 invented indirect control or is uniquely free of micromanagement.

**Tradeoffs and alternative explanations.** Delegation might feel satisfying, opaque or unresponsive. Attraction could come from the animated globs rather than decision-making. A generic RTS audience may expect direct selection and immediate attack commands; a “no micro” shortcut would hide a central learning task. This candidate does not promise shorter matches, easier victories or lower cognitive load.

**Unresolved test.** Can viewers explain what the player does, predict a demand change and later explain an unmet demand? Does that control remain appealing after they experience the delay and tradeoff? Compare an unaided response to the pitch with response after the same neutral control demonstration; distinguish misunderstanding from dislike of the actual mechanic.

## B — Make the colony work

**Audience and occasion.** A colony/logistics player wants the satisfaction of diagnosing a shortage and restoring a functioning system. The job is to make several competing needs work together, within a rival-colony match rather than an assumed endless personal settlement.

**Draft headline:** “Food, workers, expansion. Make the colony work.”

**Draft short pitch:** “Build and sustain a colony of autonomous globs. Set work priorities, keep food and training in balance, and expand while rival colonies pursue their own goals. Globulation 2 turns the colony economy into a real-time strategy contest.”

**Evidence and proposed proof.** [P02–P04](../facts/product.md#gameplay-and-player-surfaces) document food/population/service tradeoffs, rival objectives and solo learning. [Guide chapters and gameplay imagery](../facts/assets.md#screenshots-recordings-and-educational-media) supply possible material for a food/labor example. Use matching game state and captions; the [A11 replay views](../facts/assets.md#screenshots-recordings-and-educational-media) have disabled fog and cropped HUD, so alone they cannot show an ordinary player's decision information.

**Value hypothesis.** The appealing loop may be diagnosing needs and seeing a living workforce respond, with opponents supplying pressure. This is a narrower claim than the narrative/relationship depth of RimWorld or the production engineering of Factorio; [comparables](comparables.md) records those differences.

**Tradeoffs and alternative explanations.** “Colony” may create expectations of persistent characters, peaceful construction, roguelite progression or a long technology tree. A viewer may enjoy the look of a thriving colony while disliking hunger, defeat and constrained labor. Interest in optimization does not establish interest in Glob2's particular abstractions or match pacing.

**Unresolved test.** Ask what persists, what can end the game and where conflict enters. Then observe a verified shortage scenario and whether diagnosing it is satisfying. If enthusiasm depends on a persistent or combat-free experience, record a mismatch rather than counting the initial positive reaction as positioning success.

## C — Strategy you can keep

**Audience and occasion.** A player finds a free-software/native-game project and wants to try a self-contained strategy game with local control over access. Returning players may share the circumstance, but neither Linux use nor nostalgia establishes the motivation.

**Draft headline:** “A free, open-source colony RTS.”

**Draft short pitch:** “Direct a colony of autonomous globs through work priorities, food, training and expansion. Globulation 2 has a long open-source history, experimental native downloads and documented solo and offline/LAN play. Browser play is another way to explore the game.”

**Evidence and proposed proof.** The [distribution ledger](../facts/distribution.md) records current browser entry, native experimental downloads and differing package lineages. [P04–P07](../facts/product.md#gameplay-and-player-surfaces) document solo/offline/LAN/client capabilities. [History](../facts/history.md) supplies continuity evidence. A selected native build still needs an actual offline-install/play check before a platform-specific public promise; source documentation alone does not complete this gate.

**Value hypothesis.** Free-software access and native/local play may remove an objection or create trust. They might also be a supporting reason rather than the reason someone wants to play. The headline deliberately avoids “free forever,” “no account ever,” universal device support or “everything is free.”

**Tradeoffs and alternative explanations.** Optional account/web AI services and cosmetic entitlements are documented in [B01–B05](../facts/product.md#existing-commercial-machinery); live availability is not established. The free game must not be conflated with every optional hosted service. Browser saves are profile-local according to the [existing save evidence](jobs-to-be-done.md), rather than a cross-device continuity promise. Approval of open-source values may produce no playing, contribution or payment behavior.

**Unresolved test.** Ask separately why the game sounds enjoyable and why its access model matters. Verify the participant's chosen install/play route, save expectations and understanding of optional services. Compare responses before and after showing the same core gameplay description; do not attribute interest to licensing just because it was mentioned first.

## D — Give your strategy a bot

**Audience and occasion.** A JavaScript/code tinkerer wants a visible environment for testing an authored opponent. The job is to turn a strategic idea into behavior, diagnose it and improve it. This addresses a creator entry point; it is not a replacement explanation for people who simply want to play.

**Draft headline:** “Write an opponent. Watch your strategy play out.”

**Draft short pitch:** “Explore Globulation 2's JavaScript AI tools: author an opponent for an autonomous-colony RTS and inspect what it does in a match. The source and starter project provide an entry point for experimenting with strategy as code.”

**Publication gate.** This pitch is **conditional review copy** until the exact starter/API workflow has been exercised against a named current build. It promises neither public publishing on the deployed service nor an enabled paid coding Studio. If that gate is not met, use it only to recruit for a transparent prototype/workflow test.

**Evidence and proposed proof.** [P11–P12](../facts/product.md#creator-and-community-content-estate) and the public AI starter in [repositories](../facts/repositories.md) document authored AI, validation and coding paths. A credible demonstration would retain the authored change, build/API identity, test setup and visible behavior. It has not been produced in this phase. [Screeps comparison](comparables.md#code-and-creator-adjacency) clarifies that discrete authored matches are not a persistent programmable MMO.

**Value hypothesis.** Seeing an agent make strategic decisions could give code experiments a tangible consequence. This differs from generative map/music tools, an AI commander used by players and ordinary built-in computer opponents. The word “AI” should not merge these jobs.

**Tradeoffs and alternative explanations.** API learning, debugging, trust boundaries and playtest setup may dominate the experience. A coding participant may want learning or control, not public reach or paid assistance. Public libraries do not establish active creators, feedback, tournaments, marketplace revenue or an audience for published work. No creator-income claim is made.

**Unresolved test.** Can a participant explain the edit/test loop, make one intentional bounded change and locate evidence that it behaved as intended? What existing tool would they otherwise use? Record whether they want to return to code, play, publish or none of those; those outcomes are separate.

## Shared copy and asset boundaries

- These alternatives change the lead promise, not the underlying product facts. No option is approved for a platform listing or website update.
- Keep match pressure visible when testing colony imagery. Do not infer “cozy,” no combat, persistent colonies, short sessions, competitive population, co-op campaigns, flawless automation or accessibility from the pictures or control model.
- Treat browser entry, experimental native distribution, hosted services, creator publication and current device support as separate paths. Check the named path before copy approval.
- Label replay/spectator captures and experimental content by context. Do not test one option with polished footage and another with a weak or unrelated screenshot and call the difference a message result.
- Core game access, optional purchases, authored bots, generation and AI-assisted command need separate explanations. Prices, margins and payer demand remain outside the evidence collected here.
