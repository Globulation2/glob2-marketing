# Jobs-to-be-done possibility map

[Analysis index](README.md) · [Audience hypotheses](audiences.md) · [Product evidence](../facts/product.md) · [Channels and business models](channels-and-business-models.md) · [Comparable games](comparables.md)

Written **26 October 2026**. These are **hypothetical jobs**, framed as circumstances, progress and outcomes. They are not quotations from research participants, validated demand, feature commitments or recommendations. They overlap: the same person can switch jobs between sessions. Product references P01–P16 and B01–B05 refer to the [factual capability ledger](../facts/product.md); live service availability remains a separate question.

Save-related references below rely on the pinned game [browser-storage guide](https://github.com/Globulation2/glob2/blob/c9198b854a2ea6fc16cd5524913cf96c5a80c499/docs/browser/storage.md) and the website's [prior content-verification record](https://github.com/Globulation2/glob2-online-website/blob/6e391ebe93b725747827a3ed7cea78f0d0d8f7e1/docs/website-content-verification.md). They document profile/origin-local browser storage and export/backup behavior; this pass did not retest successful save/export/reload on current deployed clients.

## Player jobs

| ID | Circumstance and desired progress | Functional, emotional and social outcomes that might matter | Evidence connection / unresolved fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| J01 | When a conventional RTS feels dominated by controlling individual units, I want to direct a whole colony's priorities so I can enjoy strategic consequences | Functional: requests produce understandable responses. Emotional: feel capable rather than overwhelmed. Social: explain a good decision to another player | P01–P03 support indirect control. Whether this reduces unwanted execution burden or simply introduces unfamiliar complexity is unmeasured. |
| J02 | When I want an interconnected problem to solve, I want to stabilize a struggling colony so I can see why a change worked | Diagnose shortage, restore food/hauling, understand tradeoffs; satisfaction from a functioning system | P02/P04, guides and actual HUD captures supply possible feedback. Diagnosability and enjoyment of failure have not been studied. |
| J03 | When I have an uncertain amount of free time, I want to try an understandable game and preserve progress so I can return without losing work | Low entry effort, clear next step, successful save/load and confidence leaving | Browser/tutorial/offline capabilities exist; browser saves are profile-local. Match length, tutorial time and cross-device continuity are not established. “Instant browser entry” does not fulfill the complete job. |
| J04 | When I want to improve at a strategy game, I want a fair challenge and intelligible feedback so I can distinguish skill gains from luck or system failure | Appropriate opponent, understandable result, inspectable mistakes, credible progress; recognition without embarrassment | AI opponents, rankings and match history exist in P04/P06. Opponent availability, replay journey, balance and perceived AI-assistance fairness remain unknown. |
| J05 | When friends want something to do together, I want everyone to reach the same match so our limited shared time goes into play | Invite works, setup is explainable, versions/devices cooperate, mixed experience levels have a role | P05 contains rooms, guests, invites and configurable seats. A dedicated co-op campaign, drop-in play or progression shared across sessions is not established. Competitive social play and cooperative goals are separate variants. |
| J06 | When I want a self-contained activity without relying on hosted accounts, I want to play and retain local files so I can control access and continuity | Offline availability, reproducible installation, local saves and understandable ownership; autonomy/trust | Source, native builds, offline/LAN and save export are relevant facts. Installed platform qualifications and actual desired degree of technical control vary; not every free-software user wants to compile or self-host. |
| J07 | When I rediscover a game from years ago, I want to recognize it and understand what changed so I can reconnect without obsolete expectations | Identify current version, distinguish historical advice, locate compatible content or community; continuity and belonging | [History](../facts/history.md), wiki and current website establish a historical estate. Old account/content portability and current returning-player demand were not verified. |
| J08 | When I am curious about autonomous systems, I want to watch a living colony respond to interventions so I can enjoy and understand emergent behavior | Legible cause/effect, useful camera/control feedback, memorable moments; curiosity and delight | P01–P02 and [actual colony media](../facts/assets.md) show possible ingredients. Warfare, hunger and active management may conflict with an observer's imagined relaxed experience. |

## Paid-service jobs: separate from willingness to pay

| Possible circumstance | Desired progress | Existing mechanism | Reasons the job might remain unfulfilled |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I have a concrete creative idea but lack some production skill | Get an editable, playable or listenable result without losing control of the intent | Optional map/art/music/code generation and domain wallets B03–B04 | Output mismatch, uncertain credits/cost, repeated repair, rights concerns, trust and tool skill can erase time savings. No willingness-to-pay evidence collected. |
| I understand my strategic goal but want help expressing or maintaining commands | Delegate bounded commander work while retaining manual agency | Hive Mind B02 | It may remove enjoyable decisions, create spending anxiety or affect fairness expectations. Standing orders and paid supervision have different behavior; enabled instance/qualification remains prerequisite. |
| I want my colony to feel personal | Obtain or make a recognizable appearance | One-time presets/designer machinery B01 | Default appearance may already satisfy the need; value, live price and checkout availability unverified. |
| I value the project's continued existence | Contribute support in a way I understand and trust | A supporter job is plausible; no general donation/support program established by this inventory | Unclear allocation, preference for volunteering, lack of an appropriate mechanism or no budget. A motive to support is distinct from purchasing a paid feature. |

A job can be real while a payment offer is wrong, unavailable or unnecessary. Free alternatives, manual authoring, other games and doing nothing are possible substitutes; their relative appeal has not been measured. No business-model choice follows from the table.

## Forces that could shape adoption and continued use

| Journey circumstance | Push or attraction hypothesis | Anxiety or habit hypothesis | Evidence needed to tell them apart |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First encounter | Intriguing autonomous colony, recognizable history, unusual strategic control | Can't tell what the player actually does; expects a different genre | What viewers believe they will do after seeing a specific screenshot or description; actual first actions after entry |
| First session | Tutorial, no-install browser entry, visible colony response | Unclear UI, work delays, food failure, device/performance issues | Observed completion path, error/failure explanation and where expectations diverge |
| First return | Unfinished challenge, new opponent, own creation or a friend invite | Lost save, forgotten controls, no opponent or another familiar game | Reason and circumstance for returning versus leaving; successful restore and time available |
| Social entry | Shared activity and friendly rivalry | Coordinating versions/accounts/time, skill gap or fear of being a burden | Whether an invited group reaches a match and how roles are understood |
| First creation | Express an idea and see it run | Tooling/API complexity, uncertain rights, no audience | Time/effort to first valid useful result, quality judgment and publication/feedback experience |
| First purchase | Predictable convenience, expression or support | Cost uncertainty, dislike of monetization, output trust or fairness concerns | Comprehension of offer and charges, perceived value, reasons to decline, refund/support experience |

These are alternative explanations, not measured funnel loss or causal findings. A website visit, an account-day, a library download and a payment are different units. The [measurement ledger](../facts/product.md#measurement-and-account-infrastructure) records implemented reporting and its limits; existing counters do not reveal motivation by themselves.

## Outcome vocabulary and open questions

Possible outcomes include time until the player understands worker demand, whether a stopped task can be diagnosed, whether a food shortage can be recovered, whether a player saves and successfully returns, whether invited friends reach one game, whether an author produces an intentionally useful result, and whether a purchaser understands a credit charge. These are examples of observables, **not selected KPIs or success thresholds**.

Relevant questions remain: What progress was the person trying to make before discovering Glob2? What did they use instead? Why try it at that moment? What made them hesitate or stop? Which part felt satisfying or burdensome? What would they miss if they could not return? For creators, does success mean personal expression, learning, audience, collaboration or income? For payers, which benefit is valuable enough to exchange money rather than time or effort?

No job, persona, channel, outcome metric or offer has been prioritized. This map records the possibility space for later evidence and decisions.
